10. Be it enacted by the General Assembly of Virginia:
11. 1. That the Code of Virginia is amended by adding in Title 59.1 a chapter numbered 52, consisting
12. of sections numbered 59.1-571 through 59.1-581, as follows:
13. CHAPTER 52.
14. CONSUMER DATA PROTECTION ACT.
15. **§ 59.1-571. Definitions.**
16. As used in this chapter, unless the context requires a different meaning:
17. "Affiliate" means a legal entity that controls, is controlled by, or shares common control with
18. another legal entity. For the purposes of this definition, "control" or "controlled" means (i) ownership
19. of, or the power to vote, more than 50 percent of the outstanding shares of any class of voting security
20. of a company; (ii) control in any manner over the election of a majority of the directors or of
21. individuals exercising similar functions; or (iii) the power to exercise controlling influence over the
22. management of a company.
23. "Authenticate" means verifying through reasonable means that the consumer, entitled to exercise his
24. consumer rights in § 59.1-573, is the same consumer exercising such consumer rights with respect to the
25. personal data at issue.
26. "Business associate" means the same meaning as the term established by HIPAA.
27. "Child" means any natural person younger than 13 years of age.
28. "Consent" means a clear affirmative act signifying a consumer's freely given, specific, informed, and
29. unambiguous agreement to process personal data relating to the consumer. Consent may include a
30. written statement, including a statement written by electronic means, or any other unambiguous
31. affirmative action.
32. "Consumer" means a natural person who is a resident of the Commonwealth acting only in an
33. individual or household context. It does not include a natural person acting in a commercial or
34. employment context.
35. "Controller" means the natural or legal entity that, alone or jointly with others, determines the
36. purpose and means of processing personal data.
37. "Covered entity" means the same as the term is established by HIPAA.
38. "Decisions that produce legal or similarly significant effects concerning a consumer" means a
39. decision made by the controller that results in the provision or denial by the controller of financial and
40. lending services, housing, insurance, education enrollment, criminal justice, employment opportunities,
41. health care services, or access to basic necessities, such as food and water.
42. "De-identified data" means data that cannot reasonably be linked to an identified or identifiable
43. natural person, or a device linked to such person. A controller that possesses "de-identified data" shall
44. comply with the requirements of subsection A of § 59.1-577.
45. "Fund" means the Consumer Privacy Fund established pursuant to § 59.1-581.
46. "Health record" means the same as that term is defined in § 32.1-127.1:03.
47. "Health care provider" means the same as that term is defined in § 32.1-276.3.
48. "HIPAA" means the federal Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996 (42 U.S.C.
49. § 1320d et seq.).
50. "Identified or identifiable natural person" means a person who can be readily identified, directly or
51. indirectly.
52. "Personal data" means any information that is linked or reasonably associated to an identified or
53. identifiable natural person. "Personal data" does not include de-identified data or publicly available
54. information.
55. "Precise geolocation data" means information derived from technology, including but not limited to
56. global positioning system level latitude and longitude coordinates or other mechanisms, that directly
57. identifies the specific location of a natural person with precision and accuracy below 1,750 feet.
58. "Precise geolocation data" does not include the content of communications.
59. "Process" or "processing" means any operation or set of operations performed, whether by manual
60. or automated means, on personal data or on sets of personal data, such as the collection, use, storage,
61. disclosure, analysis, deletion, or modification of personal data.
62. "Processor" means a natural or legal entity that processes personal data on behalf of a controller.
63. "Profiling" means any form of automated processing performed on personal data to evaluate,
64. analyze, or predict personal aspects related to an identified or identifiable natural person's economic
65. situation, health, personal preferences, interests, reliability, behavior, location, or movements.
66. "Protected health information" means the same as the term is established by HIPAA.
67. "Pseudonymous data" means personal data that cannot be attributed to a specific natural person
68. without the use of additional information, provided that such additional information is kept separately
69. and is subject to appropriate technical and organizational measures to ensure that the personal data is
70. not attributed to an identified or identifiable natural person.
71. "Publicly available information" means information that is lawfully made available through federal,
72. state, or local government records, or information that a business has a reasonable basis to believe is
73. lawfully made available to the general public through widely distributed media, by the consumer, or by
74. a person to whom the consumer has disclosed the information, unless the consumer has restricted the
75. information to a specific audience.
76. "Sale of personal data" means the exchange of personal data for monetary consideration by the
77. controller to a third party. "Sale of personal data" does not include:
78. 1. The disclosure of personal data to a processor that processes the personal data on behalf of the
79. controller;
80. 2. The disclosure of personal data to a third party with whom the consumer has a direct relationship
81. for purposes of providing a product or service requested by the consumer;
82. 3. The disclosure or transfer of personal data to an affiliate of the controller;
83. 4. The disclosure of information that the consumer (i) intentionally made available to the general
84. public via a channel of mass media and (ii) did not restrict to a specific audience; or
85. 5. The disclosure or transfer of personal data to a third party as an asset that is part of a merger,
86. acquisition, bankruptcy, or other transaction in which the third party assumes control of all or part of
87. the controller's assets.
88. "Sensitive data" means a category of personal data that includes:
89. 1. Personal data revealing racial or ethnic origin, religious beliefs, mental or physical health
90. diagnosis, sexual orientation, or citizenship or immigration status;
91. 2. The processing of genetic or biometric data for the purpose of uniquely identifying a natural
92. person;
93. 3. The personal data collected from a known child; or
94. 4. Precise geolocation data.
95. "State agency" means the same as that term is defined in § 2.2-307.
96. "Targeted advertising" means displaying advertisements to a consumer where the advertisement is
97. selected based on personal data obtained from a consumer's activities over time and across nonaffiliated
98. websites or online applications to predict such consumer's preferences or interests. "Targeted
99. advertising" does not include:
100. 1. Advertisements based on activities within a controller's own websites or online applications;
101. 2. Advertisements based on the context of a consumer's current search query, visit to a website, or
102. online application;
103. 3. Advertisements directed to a consumer in response to the consumer's request for information or
104. feedback; or
105. 4. Processing personal data processed solely for measuring or reporting advertising performance,
106. reach, or frequency.
107. "Third party" means a natural or legal person, public authority, agency, or body other than the
108. consumer, controller, processor, or an affiliate of the processor or the controller.
109. **§ 59.1-572. Scope; exemptions.**
110. A. This chapter applies to persons that conduct business in the Commonwealth or produce products
111. or services that are targeted to residents of the Commonwealth and that (i) during a calendar year,
112. control or process personal data of at least 100,000 consumers or (ii) control or process personal data
113. of at least 25,000 consumers and derive over 50 percent of gross revenue from the sale of personal
114. data.
115. B. This chapter shall not apply to (i) any body, authority, board, bureau, commission, district, or
116. agency of the Commonwealth or of any political subdivision of the Commonwealth; (ii) financial
117. institutions or data subject to Title V of the federal Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act (15 U.S.C. § 6801 et seq.);
118. or (iii) any covered entity or business associate governed by the privacy, security, and breach
119. notification rules issued by the United States Department of Health and Human Services, 45 C.F.R.
120. Parts 160 and 164 established pursuant to HIPAA, and the Health Information Technology for
121. Economic and Clinical Health Act (Public Law 111-5).
122. C. The following information and data is exempt from this chapter:
123. 1. Protected health information under HIPAA;
124. 2. Health records for purposes of Title 32.1;
125. 3. Patient identifying information for purposes of 42 U.S.C. § 290dd-2;
126. 4. Identifiable private information for purposes of the federal policy for the protection of human
127. subjects under 45 C.F.R. Part 46; identifiable private information that is otherwise information collected
128. as part of human subjects research pursuant to the good clinical practice guidelines issued by The
129. International Council for Harmonisation of Technical Requirements for Pharmaceuticals for Human
130. Use; the protection of human subjects under 21 C.F.R. Parts 6, 50, and 56; or personal data used or
131. shared in research conducted in accordance with the requirements set forth in this chapter;
132. 5. Information and documents created for purposes of the federal Health Care Quality Improvement
133. Act of 1986 (42 U.S.C. § 11101 et seq.);
134. 6. Patient safety work product for purposes of the federal Patient Safety and Quality Improvement
135. Act (42 U.S.C. § 299b-21 et seq.);
136. 7. Information derived from any of the health care-related information listed in this subsection that is
137. de-identified in accordance with the requirements for de-identification pursuant to HIPAA;
138. 8. Information originating from, and intermingled to be indistinguishable with, information exempt
139. under this subsection that is maintained by a covered entity or business associate as defined by HIPAA
140. or a program or a qualified service organization as defined by 42 U.S.C. § 290dd-2;
141. 9. Information used only for public health activities and purposes as authorized by HIPAA;
142. 10. The collection, maintenance, disclosure, sale, communication, or use of any personal information
143. bearing on a consumer's credit worthiness, credit standing, credit capacity, character, general
144. reputation, personal characteristics, or mode of living by a consumer reporting agency, furnisher, or
145. user that provides information for use in a consumer report, and by a user of a consumer report, but
146. only to the extent that such activity is regulated by and authorized under the federal Fair Credit
147. Reporting Act (15 U.S.C. § 1681 et seq.);
148. 11. Personal data collected, processed, sold, or disclosed in compliance with the federal Driver's
149. Privacy Protection Act of 1994 (18 U.S.C. § 2721 et seq.);
150. 12. Personal data regulated by the federal Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act (20 U.S.C. §
151. 1232g et seq.);
152. 13. Personal data collected, processed, sold, or disclosed in compliance with the federal Farm Credit
153. Act (12 U.S.C. § 2001 et seq.); and
154. 14. Data processed by a controller, processor, or third party (i) in the course of an individual
155. applying to, employed by, or acting as an agent of a controller, processor, or third party, to the extent
156. that the data is collected and used within the context of that role; (ii) as the emergency contact
157. information of an individual under this chapter used for emergency contact purposes; or (iii) that is
158. necessary for the controller, processor, or third party to retain to administer benefits for another
159. individual relating to the individual under clause (i) and used for the purposes of administering those
160. benefits.
161. D. Controllers and processors that comply with the verifiable parental consent requirements of the
162. Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (15 U.S.C. § 6501 et seq.) shall be deemed compliant with any
163. obligation to obtain parental consent under this chapter.
164. **§ 59.1-573. Personal data rights; consumers.**
165. A. A consumer may invoke the consumer rights authorized pursuant to this subsection at any time by
166. submitting a request to a controller specifying the consumer rights the consumer wishes to invoke. A
167. known child's parent or legal guardian may invoke such consumer rights on behalf of the child
168. regarding processing personal data belonging to the known child. A controller shall comply with an
169. authenticated consumer request to exercise the right:
170. 1. To confirm whether or not a controller is processing his personal data and to access such
171. personal data;
172. 2. To correct inaccuracies in his personal data, taking into account the nature of the personal data
173. and the purposes of the processing of the personal data;
174. 3. To delete his personal data;
175. 4. To obtain a copy of his personal data that he previously provided to the controller in a portable
176. and, to the extent technically feasible, readily usable format that allows the consumer to transmit the
177. data to another controller without hindrance, where the processing is carried out by automated means;
178. and
179. 5. To opt out of the processing of the personal data for purposes of targeted advertising, the sale of
180. personal data, or profiling in furtherance of decisions that produce legal or similarly significant effects
181. concerning the consumer.
182. B. Except as otherwise provided in this chapter, a controller shall comply with a request by a
183. consumer to exercise the consumer rights authorized pursuant to subsection A as follows:
184. 1. A controller shall respond to the consumer without undue delay, but in all cases within 45 days of
185. receipt of the request. The response period may be extended once by 45 additional days when
186. reasonably necessary, taking into account the complexity and number of the consumer's requests, so
187. long as the controller informs the consumer of any such extension within the initial 45-day response
188. period, together with the reasons for the extension.
189. 2. If a controller declines to take action regarding the consumer's request, the controller shall inform
190. the consumer without undue delay, but in all cases and at the latest within 45 days of receipt of the
191. request, of the justification for declining to take action and instructions for how to appeal the decision
192. pursuant to subsection C.
193. 3. Information provided in response to a consumer request shall be provided by a controller free of
194. charge, up to twice annually per consumer. If requests from a consumer are manifestly unfounded,
195. excessive, or repetitive, the controller may charge the consumer a reasonable fee to cover the
196. administrative costs of complying with the request or decline to act on the request. The controller bears
197. the burden of demonstrating the manifestly unfounded, excessive, or repetitive nature of the request.
198. 4. If a controller is unable to fulfill the request using commercially reasonable efforts, the controller
199. shall not be required to comply with a request to initiate an action under subsection A and may request
200. that the consumer provide additional information reasonably necessary to authenticate the request.
201. C. A controller shall establish a process for a consumer to appeal the controller's refusal to take
202. action on a request within a reasonable period of time after the consumer's receipt of the decision
203. pursuant to subdivision B 2. The appeal process shall be conspicuously available and similar to the
204. process for submitting requests to initiate action pursuant to subsection A. Within 60 days of receipt of
205. an appeal, a controller shall inform the consumer in writing of any action taken or not taken in
206. response to the appeal, including a written explanation of the reasons for the decisions. The controller
207. shall also provide the consumer with an email address or other online mechanism through which the
208. consumer may contact the Attorney General to submit a complaint.
209. **§ 59.1-574. Data controller responsibilities; transparency.**
210. A. A controller shall:
211. 1. Limit the collection of personal data to what is adequate, relevant, and reasonably necessary in
212. relation to the purposes for which such data is processed, as disclosed to the consumer;
213. 2. Except as otherwise provided in this chapter, not process personal data for purposes not
214. reasonably necessary to, or compatible with, the disclosed purposes for which such personal data is
215. processed, as disclosed to the consumer, unless the controller obtains the consumer's consent;
216. 3. Establish, implement, and maintain reasonable administrative, technical, and physical data
217. security practices to protect the confidentiality, integrity, and accessibility of personal data. Such data
218. security practices shall be appropriate to the volume and nature of the personal data at issue;
219. 4. Not process personal data in violation of state and federal laws that prohibit unlawful
220. discrimination against consumers. A controller shall not discriminate against a consumer for exercising
221. any of the consumer rights contained in this chapter, including denying goods or services, charging
222. different prices or rates for goods or services, or providing a different level of quality of goods and
223. services to the consumer. However, nothing in this subdivision shall be construed to prohibit a
224. controller from offering a different price, rate, level, quality, or selection of goods or services to a
225. consumer, including offering goods or services for no fee, if the consumer has opted out of targeted
226. advertising or the offer is related to a consumer's voluntary participation in a bona fide loyalty,
227. rewards, premium features, discounts, or club card program; and
228. 5. Not process sensitive data concerning a consumer without obtaining the consumer's consent, or, in
229. the case of the processing of sensitive data concerning a known child, without processing such data in
230. accordance with the federal Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (15 U.S.C. § 6501 et seq.).
231. B. Any provision of a contract or agreement of any kind that purports to waive or limit in any way
232. consumer rights pursuant to § 59.1-573 shall be deemed contrary to public policy and shall be void and
233. unenforceable.
234. C. Controllers shall provide consumers with a reasonably accessible, clear, and meaningful privacy
235. notice that includes:
236. 1. The categories of personal data processed by the controller;
237. 2. The purpose for processing personal data;
238. 3. How consumers may exercise their consumer rights pursuant § 59.1-573, including how a
239. consumer may appeal a controller's decision with regard to the consumer's request;
240. 4. The categories of personal data that the controller shares with third parties, if any; and
241. 5. The categories of third parties, if any, with whom the controller shares personal data.
242. D. If a controller sells personal data to third parties or processes personal data for targeted
243. advertising, the controller shall clearly and conspicuously disclose such processing, as well as the
244. manner in which a consumer may exercise the right to opt out of such processing.
245. E. A controller shall establish, and shall describe in a privacy notice, one or more secure and
246. reliable means for consumers to submit a request to exercise their consumer rights under this chapter.
247. Such means shall take into account the ways in which consumers normally interact with the controller,
248. the need for secure and reliable communication of such requests, and the ability of the controller to
249. authenticate the identity of the consumer making the request. Controllers shall not require a consumer
250. to create a new account in order to exercise consumer rights pursuant to § 59.1-573 but may require a
251. consumer to use an existing account.
252. **§ 59.1-575. Responsibility according to role; controller and processor.**
253. A. A processor shall adhere to the instructions of a controller and shall assist the controller in
254. meeting its obligations under this chapter. Such assistance shall include:
255. 1. Taking into account the nature of processing by appropriate technical and organizational
256. measures, insofar as possible, to fulfill the controller's obligation to respond to consumer rights requests
257. pursuant to § 59.1-573;
258. 2. Securely processing personal data, taking into account the nature of processing information
259. available to the processor, and complying with security breach notification requirements pursuant to §
260. 18.2-186.6 in order to meet the controller's obligations; and
261. 3. Providing necessary information to enable the controller to conduct and document data protection
262. assessments pursuant to § 59.1-576.
263. B. A contract between a controller and a processor shall govern the processor's data processing
264. procedures. The contract shall be binding and clearly set forth instructions for processing data, the
265. nature and purpose of processing, the type of data subject to processing, the duration of processing, and
266. the rights and obligations of both parties. The contract shall also include requirements that:
267. 1. The processor shall ensure that each person processing personal data is subject to a duty of
268. confidentiality with respect to the data;
269. 2. At the controller's discretion, the processor shall delete or return all personal data to the
270. controller as requested at the end of the provision of services, unless retention of the personal data is
271. required by law;
272. 3. The processor shall make available to the controller all information in its possession necessary to
273. demonstrate compliance with the obligations in this chapter; and
274. 4. The processor shall allow, and contribute to, reasonable audits and inspections by the controller
275. or the controller's designated auditor; alternatively, the processor may arrange for a qualified and
276. independent auditor to conduct an audit of the processor's policies and technical and organizational
277. measures in support of the obligations under this chapter using an appropriate and accepted control
278. standard or framework and audit procedure for such audits. The processor shall provide a report of
279. such audit to the controller upon request.
280. C. Nothing in this section shall be construed to relieve a controller or a processor from the
281. liabilities imposed on it by virtue of its role in the processing relationship as defined by this chapter.
282. D. Determining whether a person is acting as a controller or processor with respect to a specific
283. processing of data is a fact-based determination that depends upon the context in which personal data is
284. to be processed. A processor that continues to adhere to a controller's instructions with respect to a
285. specific processing of personal data remains a processor.
286. **§ 59.1-576. Data protection assessments.**
287. A. A controller shall conduct and document a data protection assessment of each of the following
288. processing activities involving personal data:
289. 1. The processing of personal data for purposes of targeted advertising;
290. 2. The sale of personal data;
291. 3. The processing of personal data for purposes of profiling, where such profiling presents a
292. reasonably foreseeable risk of (i) unfair or deceptive treatment of, or unlawful disparate impact on,
293. consumers; (ii) financial, physical, or reputational injury to consumers; (iii) a physical or other
294. intrusion upon the solitude or seclusion, or the private affairs or concerns, of consumers, where such
295. intrusion would be offensive to a reasonable person; or (iv) other substantial injury to consumers;
296. 4. The processing of sensitive data; and
297. 5. Any processing activities involving personal data that present a heightened risk of harm to
298. consumers. A single data protection assessment may address a comparable set of processing operations
299. that include similar activities.
300. Such data protection assessments shall take into account the extent to which the personal data is
301. sensitive data and the context in which the personal data is to be processed.
302. B. Data protection assessments conducted pursuant to subsection A shall identify and weigh the
303. benefits that may flow, directly and indirectly, from the processing to the controller, the consumer, other
304. stakeholders, and the public against the potential risks to the rights of the consumer associated with
305. such processing, as mitigated by safeguards that can be employed by the controller to reduce such risks.
306. The use of de-identified data and the reasonable expectations of consumers, as well as the context of the
307. processing and the relationship between the controller and the consumer whose personal data will be
308. processed, shall be factored into this assessment by the controller.
309. C. The Attorney General may request, in writing, that a controller disclose any data protection
310. assessment that is relevant to an investigation conducted by the Attorney General, and the controller
311. shall make the data protection assessment available to the Attorney General. The Attorney General may
312. evaluate the data protection assessment for compliance with the responsibilities set forth in § 59.1-574.
313. Data protection assessments shall be confidential and exempt from public inspection and copying under
314. the Virginia Freedom of Information Act (§ 2.2-3700 et seq.). The disclosure of a data protection
315. assessment pursuant to a request from the Attorney General shall not constitute a waiver of
316. attorney-client privilege or work product protection with respect to the assessment and any information
317. contained in the assessment.
318. D. Data protection assessments conducted by a controller for the purpose of compliance with other
319. laws or regulations may comply under this section if the assessments have a reasonably comparable
320. scope and effect.
321. E. Data protection assessment requirements shall apply to processing activities created or generated
322. after January 1, 2023, and are not retroactive.
323. **§ 59.1-577. Processing de-identified data; exemptions.**
324. A. The controller in possession of de-identified data shall:
325. 1. Take reasonable measures to ensure that the data cannot be associated with a natural person;
326. 2. Publicly commit to maintaining and using de-identified data without attempting to re-identify the
327. data; and
328. 3. Contractually obligate any recipients of the de-identified data to comply with all provisions of this
329. chapter.
330. B. Nothing in this chapter shall be construed to (i) require a controller or processor to re-identify
331. de-identified data or (ii) maintain data in identifiable form, or collect, obtain, retain, or access any data
332. or technology, in order to be capable of associating an authenticated consumer request with personal
333. data.
334. C. Nothing in this chapter shall be construed to require a controller or processor to comply with an
335. authenticated consumer rights request, pursuant to § 59.1-574, if all of the following are true:
336. 1. The controller is not reasonably capable of associating the request with the personal data or it
337. would be unreasonably burdensome for the controller to associate the request with the personal data;
338. 2. The controller does not use the personal data to recognize or respond to the specific consumer
339. who is the subject of the personal data, or associate the personal data with other personal data about
340. the same specific consumer; and
341. 3. The controller does not sell the personal data to any third party or otherwise voluntarily disclose
342. the personal data to any third party other than a processor, except as otherwise permitted in this
343. section.
344. D. The consumer rights contained in § 59.1-574 shall not apply to pseudonymous data in cases
345. where the controller is able to demonstrate that any information necessary to identify the consumer is
346. kept separately and is subject to effective technical and organizational controls that prevent the
347. controller from accessing such information.
348. E. A controller that uses pseudonymous data or de-identified data shall exercise reasonable oversight
349. to monitor compliance with any contractual commitments to which the pseudonymous data or
350. de-identified data is subject and shall take appropriate steps to address any breaches of contractual
351. commitments.
352. **§ 59.1-578. Limitations.**
353. A. Nothing in this section shall be construed to restrict a controller's or processor's ability to:
354. 1. Comply with federal, state, or local laws, rules, or regulations;
355. 2. Comply with a civil, criminal, or regulatory inquiry, investigation, subpoena, or summons by
356. federal, state, local, or other governmental authorities;
357. 3. Cooperate with law-enforcement agencies concerning conduct or activity that the controller or
358. processor reasonably and in good faith believes may violate federal, state, or local laws, rules, or
359. regulations;
360. 4. Investigate, establish, exercise, prepare for, or defend legal claims;
361. 5. Provide a product or service specifically requested by a consumer, perform a contract to which
362. the consumer is a party, or take steps at the request of the consumer prior to entering into a contract;
363. 6. Take immediate steps to protect an interest that is essential for the life of the consumer or of
364. another natural person, and where the processing cannot be manifestly based on another legal basis;
365. 7. Prevent, detect, protect against, or respond to security incidents, identity theft, fraud, harassment,
366. malicious or deceptive activities, or any illegal activity; preserve the integrity or security of systems; or
367. investigate, report, or prosecute those responsible for any such action;
368. 8. Engage in public or peer-reviewed scientific, historical, or statistical research in the public
369. interest that adheres to all other applicable ethics and privacy laws if the deletion of the information is
370. likely to render impossible or seriously impair the achievement of the research and the consumer has
371. provided consent; or
372. 9. Assist another controller, processor, or third party with any of the obligations under this
373. subsection.
374. B. The obligations imposed on controllers or processors under this chapter shall not restrict a
375. controller's or processor's ability to collect, use, or retain data to:
376. 1. Conduct internal research to improve or repair products, services, or technology;
377. 2. Identify and repair technical errors that impair existing or intended functionality; or
378. 3. Perform internal operations that are reasonably aligned with the expectations of the consumer
379. based on the consumer's existing relationship with the controller or are otherwise compatible with
380. processing data in furtherance of the provision of a product or service specifically requested by a
381. consumer or the performance of a contract to which the consumer is a party.
382. C. The obligations imposed on controllers or processors under this chapter shall not apply where
383. compliance by the controller or processor with this chapter would violate an evidentiary privilege under
384. the laws of the Commonwealth. Nothing in this chapter shall be construed to prevent a controller or
385. processor from providing personal data concerning a consumer to a person covered by an evidentiary
386. privilege under the laws of the Commonwealth as part of a privileged communication.
387. D. A controller or processor that discloses personal data to a third-party controller or processor, in
388. compliance with the requirements of this chapter, is not in violation of this chapter if the third-party
389. controller or processor that receives and processes such personal data is in violation of this chapter,
390. provided that, at the time of disclosing the personal data, the disclosing controller or processor did not
391. have actual knowledge that the recipient intended to commit a violation. A third-party controller or
392. processor receiving personal data from a controller or processor in compliance with the requirements of
393. this chapter is likewise not in violation of this chapter for the transgressions of the controller or
394. processor from which it receives such personal data.
395. E. Nothing in this chapter shall be construed as an obligation imposed on controllers and processors
396. that adversely affects the rights or freedoms of any persons, such as exercising the right of free speech
397. pursuant to the First Amendment to the United States Constitution, or applies to the processing of
398. personal data by a person in the course of a purely personal or household activity.
399. F. Personal data processed by a controller pursuant to this section shall not be processed for any
400. purpose other than those expressly listed in this section unless otherwise allowed by this chapter.
401. Personal data processed by a controller pursuant to this section may be processed to the extent that
402. such processing is:
403. 1. Reasonably necessary and proportionate to the purposes listed in this section; and
